# RHEL-HOD-002 Content Review

## Readiness

The advanced administrator theory reference is ready for editorial review. Its recording remains intentionally absent until provenance, permission, update, rollback, repair, and fleet-management commands have been tested on the selected RHEL release.

## Findings

- The page begins where administrator fundamentals end and does not teach application development.
- RHEL-HOD-001 is an explicit required prerequisite, and its completion record supplies the starting deployment evidence.
- Provenance includes remote, full ref, commit, origin, architecture, branch, and installation scope.
- Effective access includes static permissions, overrides, portal state, D-Bus, sockets, devices, filesystem exposure, and desktop behavior.
- Lifecycle guidance distinguishes deployment rollback, application-data migration, repair, evidence preservation, and end-of-life.
- Fleet delivery includes application, runtime, extensions, metadata, trust, credentials, mirrors, offline workflows, and Satellite.
- The enterprise operating-model and readiness decisions cover direct, centrally configured, Satellite-managed, and disconnected patterns.
- Service-account boundaries, Satellite mirroring and promotion, minimum fleet inventory, event-driven review, and symptom-led recovery are explicit.
- A concise deployment standard consolidates source, scope, authentication, permissions, updates, inventory, data, recovery, and support decisions.
- The managed Thunderbird scenario continues from the required foundation into audit, recovery, and fleet governance.
- Support routing separates RHEL, Flatpak, repositories, Satellite, runtimes, applications, and portals.

## Comparison review

- The tables support approval, auditing, incident response, and fleet operations rather than repeating fundamentals.
- Every operational claim identifies an owner, risk, or evidence requirement.
- Broad permissions and unverified sources are not normalized as troubleshooting shortcuts.
- Support statements retain the distinction between RHEL releases and third-party content.

## Learner experience

- Preview: outcomes define the evidence and controls an advanced operator should produce.
- Practice: the theory follows provenance, deployment identity, effective access, lifecycle, and support decisions.
- Prove: the self-check requires an approval and recovery explanation before recording begins.
- Three planned demonstrations separate permission auditing, update and recovery controls, and enterprise fleet delivery. The HOD does not include a dedicated next card.

## Role-based review

### Technical Support Engineer

The diagnostic set begins with the affected scope and preserves deployment, history, permission, and repository evidence before repair or rollback changes local state.

### Solution Architect / Pre-Sales

The content makes trust, credential, ownership, support, promotion, offline, and recovery boundaries visible before Flatpak is proposed as an enterprise delivery model.

### Technical Consultant

The page can be translated into an operating standard covering approved remotes, installation scope, permissions, service accounts, Satellite content completeness, promotion, change control, inventory, event-driven review, and incident response.

### Instructor

The progression moves from trust and identity through access auditing, lifecycle control, diagnostics, and recall. The recording must explain each command and attach troubleshooting to the affected step.

### Student

The page explains why an application ID is insufficient, why effective access has several layers, why rollback may not restore data compatibility, and why offline delivery needs runtime content.

### Technical Marketing Manager

The material presents enterprise control without claiming that sandboxing proves trust, signing proves application quality, or every Flatpak source has Red Hat support.

### Technical Account Manager

The support routes and ownership records provide evidence for customer escalation while keeping registry tokens, signing keys, and unrelated personal data out of diagnostics.

### Sales

The page explains the operational value of controlled desktop application delivery while keeping governance effort, third-party support boundaries, dependency ownership, and recovery limitations explicit.

RHEL-HOD-002 satisfies the theory-stage content standard, eight-role review, Preview → Practice → Prove structure, track-specific identity rule, source-boundary requirement, and support-routing requirement.
